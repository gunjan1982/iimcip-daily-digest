Note

How to read this, and what it is not

Where the lanes came from

Six lanes are the teams named for this exercise. Two more were added because the process cannot be drawn without them:

  • COO & BD — opportunities originate here, and Pranjal, Veeresh, Pankaj, Gaurav and Srinivas all do business development.
  • Leadership & IC — go/no-go, jury and investment decisions terminate here.
Where the map departs from the org chart

The supplied org chart is partly current and partly outdated, and mixes reporting lines with capabilities and programme names.

  • Vineet's Admin, HR and Compliance work sits under the Finance, Compliance & Control vertical on the chart, not beside it.
  • The chart shows a Head of Investments distinct from Finance; in practice Debapratim carries finance, compliance, HR, legal, IT and investment together.
  • Rural and Startup Programs are separate verticals on the chart and in practice, but they share the sourcing, mentoring and bootcamp machinery.
Confidence

Every box traces to a specific page of the interview scans. But these are one consultant's notes from single conversations with each lead:

  • Process steps are as described by the lead, not verified against programme files.
  • Counts, amounts and dates are working figures, not audited.
  • Nothing here has been confirmed by the people mapped. Circulating it for correction is the next step, not presenting it as settled.
What this map deliberately excludes

Items raised in the interviews that are proposals rather than current practice have been kept out of the boxes:

  • The mentor policy (two meetings a month, ₹10,000, first year free) — verbally accepted, not yet policy.
  • Dual evaluation as an IIMCIP-wide standard — currently one team's practice.
  • The four-stage immersion / pre-incubation / incubation / acceleration model — a useful frame, but IIMCIP operates mainly in incubation.

The next three things to settle before any of this is built

  • Who owns Portfolio. Sourcing and Portfolio are the same person today. Until that is decided, stage 7 has no accountable owner and the 150-startup recovery has no home.
  • One canonical investment sequence. Due diligence cannot sit both before and after the term sheet. Leadership needs to approve one order, with entry and exit criteria for IIC and EIC.
  • Which portal is the source of truth. Contest, Connect and SAMPARK each own part of a startup's record. Pick which one owns which field during transition, or the four shared records cannot be built.

Where we are, and what today's decision is

Twelve stakeholder conversations across every function, mapped end to end. The operating process works — it is carried by people who know it well and is almost entirely undocumented. That is the exposure, and it is fixable.

What you have already asked for — and where it sits in the plan

Seven of the thirty-three interventions come directly from the two of you. They are not new proposals; they are your own asks, costed and sequenced.

    The decision in front of you today

    • Which interventions are Phase 1. Thirty-three are on the table. Select and deselect as we go; the decision list at the end of this document is the minute.
    • Four of them block everything else and are marked as such: who owns Portfolio, which digital platform we build on, the data and document rules, and the permission model. Every dashboard and repository below depends on those three answers. If they slip, the rest slips.
    • Nothing here is validated yet. Every box traces to a page of the interview notes, but none of it has been confirmed by the people mapped. Circulating it for correction is the next step.
    To-Be

    The interventions — what turns As-Is into To-Be

    Thirty-three interventions, each attached to a point on the map and traced back to the master task tracker. Set each one to Phase 1, Phase 2 or Not now. The defaults are the tracker's current Phase A / B assignment — they are a starting position, not a recommendation you have to accept.

    On “permission mapping”. The term covers two different things and only one belongs in Phase 1. Approval authority — who may sign off what, at what threshold — is organisation design, already blocks the investment sequence, and is IN-32. Role-based access configuration — which role may view or edit which record — is build-time work that cannot be specified before the platform exists, and is IN-33, Phase 2. The existing FMS specification is what happens when the second is attempted before the first: a rights matrix against modules nobody has built, with a five-level serial approval chain for uploading contest documents, terminating in an approver who has since left the organisation.
